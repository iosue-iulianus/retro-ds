## Rules

**FF6-1. The logo.** The US release's splash screen lettering, "FINAL FANTASY III" (power-on, frame 500, the box 24, 60 to 237, 130 of its picture), its navy background made clear: on the second screen wherever it shows nothing else, and in the game list. A logo of the player's own wins, as for every game.

**FF6-2. The battle screens.** In battle, laid out as the DS's Chrono Trigger lays its own (pages of big buttons on the touch screen, the battle above), all in the game's own windows (the player's style and colours, the menu's gradient), font, portraits and hand cursor, drawn by RetroDS from the game's data, at 320 x 240 of the game's pixels (4 screen pixels each on the Duo's bottom screen):
- **On the game screen, the party:** where the game's battle windows were (the strip of rows 150 to 213), a window with a line for each character: name (yellow: whose turn it is), HP and its maximum, MP and its maximum (teal; for those with MP), the time gauge (the game's own pieces and colours: filling, full). Below it to the picture's bottom, black. The battle is shown once: the game's own windows are under it. Battle messages the game puts at the top of the picture (an attack's name) stay there.
- **Below, someone's turn:** top left, a window with whose turn it is (portrait, name, HP, MP, time gauge); under it, a button (a window) for each of their commands, greyed ones grey, the game's hand on the one its cursor is on; right, the enemies' names, to read, and under them Row and Def. (the game's command names; the hand on the one the pad has opened). Relm controlling a monster: its attacks in the command buttons (`$62CC` + slot; `$205E`, attack names), no Row and Def.
- **Below, choosing a target:** the same window with whose turn it is; beside it, what's being done (the command, or the spell or item chosen) and the target the cursor is on (yellow), and for a character their first status, as the game's own status window for choosing a character names it (teal; C2/ADE1, the first bit of `$2EAE` + 32 x slot + 15 to 18, Wound first); a button for every enemy alive, two across, top to bottom as they stand on the battlefield (the game's own list names each kind once: a button each lets a finger pick one); a row with a button for each character and Back. The game's hand marks the buttons the cursor covers.
- **Below, the lists** (the game's own lists, drawn: Magic with the esper first, which the game opens with Up from the top row; Item; Lore; Rage; Dance; Magitek; Throw; Tools): the same window with whose turn it is; beside it, the command and the row the game's cursor is on (yellow); a button for every row that holds something, two across and five down (the game's icon and name, the MP cost (Magic, Lore) or how many (Item, Throw); greyed: too little MP, not for battle), the game's hand on its cursor's; under them, the page's arrows (the font's up arrow, upside down for down), which page, and Back. The page opens where the game's cursor is and follows it when the pad moves it.
- **Below, Blitz:** the window of whose turn it is, the command, and a button for each Blitz Sabin knows (`$1D28`): its name and its code in the font's letters and arrows (from the ROM's codes, C4/7A40), Back. **SwdTech:** the same, the bar as a long gauge of the game's pieces (`$7B82`, 32 a level over the techniques Cyan knows), a button for each technique (CF/3C40), the game's hand on the bar's level, the one tapped yellow till the bar comes to it.
- **Below, nobody's turn** (for 20 frames: between turns the menu closes for a few, and the last page stays meanwhile): the party two by two (portraits, HP, MP, gauges) and the enemies' names.
- **As a battle starts** (about a second, while the game makes its copies of the party: the characters-shown flags `$201D` read FF, every HP FFFF), nothing is drawn: the logo below, the strip black.
- **While one of the game's own lists is open** (Slot: the game's own reels, moved over, a tap anywhere below being A; open for 4 frames, as the menu passes through other states for a frame after a target is chosen or as a list opens), the game's battle windows are shown below instead, moved over (a stopgap: the strip of its picture, where it would be were the whole picture shown as the game's is).

*(Exception to Philosophy 1, listed: the game screen's windows replaced by RetroDS's drawing of the party, where Super Metroid's mini-map is covered. The layout is the DS's Chrono Trigger's arrangement in the game's own pieces: the owner, Nov 26, 2026, with the party list on the game screen in the windows' place.)*

**FF6-3. Touch to fight.** A tap does what the game's menu does with its pad, done directly: RetroDS writes the menu's cursor where the tap was, then presses A, and the game chooses as from its own pad (its sounds, its checks, what opens next). A command's button: the command list's cursor. An enemy's or a character's button while choosing a target: the target cursor, then A; one tap, no confirming, as the DS's Chrono Trigger; only where the game's own cursor could go (its targeting rules for the action: a fixed target, a roulette or an automatic one takes A alone; an action kept to one side, only that side; only targets the game counts as there and targetable), and a target the cursor already covers (a spell on all of them) is confirmed as it is. A row of a list: its cursor (scroll, column, row, as that list has them), then A; the esper: the Magic list's top row, then Up (the game opens its esper window), a pause while it opens, A. A row of the Item list: its cursor, then A twice (the game's first A picks an item up to move it, the second on it uses it), after B if an item is already held (else the two would swap). Greyed rows can't be chosen. A Blitz: its code pressed as the player would, a button at a time (diagonals as two held together), A last: the game checks the code itself. A SwdTech: A when the bar is at its level (not in its level's last steps), the player's buttons waiting meanwhile. Slot: A. *(The owner, Nov 27, 2026, on RetroDS's recommendation: the codes and the bar done for the player, as touch suits; on the pad they're the game's as ever.)* Back is B. Row and Def.: the pad's Left or Right (L or R with the short command set), a pause while its window opens, A. X-Magic: the Magic page twice, as the game asks (the esper not offered, the game doesn't open it then). Slot: each tap A (the game's four: the draw, then each reel). A tap on the window of whose turn it is (the command page's top left) is X: the next character whose gauge is full, as on the pad. Everything else in battle (the lists not drawn yet, Row and Defend) stays on the pad for now. While the presses play (12 frames each), the player's own buttons wait. *(Writes, only these, each one logged: the command lists' cursors `$890F`-`$8912`; the battle lists' `$8913`-`$896A` (Magic, Lore, Rage, Dance, Magitek, Item, Throw, Tools: each list's own scroll, column and row, per character); the target cursor `$7B7D`-`$7B7E`. Cursor bytes the game's D-pad moves, never the game's data: what's chosen is still chosen by the game, on its A. The owner, Nov 26-27, 2026: targets by touch, Back, the next character; "is it easy enough just to send the command directly", after the disassembly showed A reads only these.)*

**FF6-4. The main menu, below.** While the game's menu program runs (the main menu on X and everything it opens: Item, Skills, Equip, Relic, Status, Config, Save; shops and the name screens, which use it too), its whole picture is the second screen's, as the game draws it, shown as the game's own picture is (its Scale); the game screen shows the logo meanwhile (as Symphony of the Night's menu page). Touch on the pages: a tap moves the game's own hand to the row tapped with the D-pad, a press at a time (2 frames down, 2 up), watching where it goes (the menu's cursor sprite, `$0055`, `$0057`; the page's spacing learnt from its moves, the nearest row taken), then presses A; sideways only where the cursor has columns (`$0053`; Config's Left and Right change a setting, so its values stay on the pad); above or below a list, nothing. A shop's number to buy or sell: a tap is A (Up and Down change the number). A swipe across is B (back). *(No writes: the game's buttons. RetroDS's judgement, Nov 27, 2026, as these pages have no room for a Back button and no reliable cursor table to write: the menu reuses its pointer.)* But the main menu itself (its commands, and choosing a character for Skills, Equip, Relic, Status): drawn by RetroDS, FF6-5. *(A stopgap for the menu's pages, until they're drawn.)*

**FF6-5. The party, drawn.** On the field and the world map, the second screen shows the party as the game's main menu shows it, drawn by RetroDS from the game's data and art: the party window with each character's portrait (the back row's further right; an imp's portrait for an imp), name, LV, HP and MP (the maximums with a relic's boost, at most 9999 and 999; MP only for those the game shows it for: any esper in the party, Gogo, or a spell known), and the Time and Steps / Gp windows, in the player's own window style and colours (Config) and font colour, with the game's gradient. With the main menu's command list (Item, Skills, Equip, Relic, Status, Config, Save; Save grey off a save point: `$1EB7` walking, `$0201` in the menu, bit 7), its window and text placed as the game's (the text 15 pixels a row, as its line scroll puts it). The same screen is the main menu while it's open (the menu program's states 05, its commands, and 06, choosing a character: `$0026`), with the game's hand on its cursor (`$004B`), and choosing a character a second hand left on the command (under the text, 2 pixels right, as the game's). Touch: a tap on a command chooses it (the main menu's cursor written, `$004B`, `$004D`, `$004E`, then A, as the game's pad would; walking, X first, then the same once the menu is open); choosing a character, a tap on them. *(Writes: the main menu's cursor bytes only, each logged; the owner's direction, Nov 27, 2026, for the menu drawn: RetroDS's judgement to keep the game's own main menu, made touchable, rather than a new layout.)* Shown as the game's own picture is (its Scale: at 4:3 the whole 4:3 screen, at Pixel Perfect whole pixels: the two screens match); drawn again only when something on it changes. Checked against the game's own menu pixel for pixel, its command list and hands too (the seven test states and choosing a character: 0 pixels differ, but for the minute's digit where the clock ticked; check-games' ff6party). Status icons (poison and the like, beside the name) aren't drawn yet. On the title, the opening, cutscenes, the ending, and with no party: the logo (FF6-1).

**FF6-6. The game above.** Until the player picks a screen for the game (Screens > Game screen, for this game or all), this game is on the first (top) screen and its windows and menu below: the DS's Chrono Trigger's way round, and the bottom screen's 4:3 takes the battle windows at full width. RetroDS's other games keep their own choice (by the screens' shapes: the Duo's bottom one).
